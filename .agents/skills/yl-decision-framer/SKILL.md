# Decision Framer

## Inputs
Accept the user's decision question and any stated objectives, stakeholders, constraints, deadline, and context. Mark missing information as missing.

## Procedure
1. Restate the decision as a choice an identifiable owner can make by a stated time. Preserve the user's language where it affects scope.
2. Separate the decision from the outcomes it is meant to influence. Define observable success measures, units, time horizon, and unacceptable outcomes where known.
3. Record constraints, affected stakeholders, dependencies, reversibility, and the cost of waiting. Include the status quo as a candidate when relevant.
4. Identify only missing facts whose answers might change the option set or the decision. Ask no more than three high-value questions; make provisional assumptions explicit if the user wants to proceed.
5. Check whether the framing embeds a preferred answer, false binary, or unexamined objective.

## Output
Return: decision statement; owner and deadline; success measures; constraints; reversibility; provisional option set; assumptions; up to three decision-changing questions. Mark each field as provided, inferred, or unknown.

## Guardrails
Do not invent authority, budget, deadline, or stakeholder agreement. Do not make the decision for the user. Do not turn a cost bracket into a measured cost.

## Trial case
Input: "Should we roll out the AI assistant?" A useful result specifies what product, which users, rollout window, owner, success and failure measures, and whether a limited pilot is possible, without fabricating their values.
