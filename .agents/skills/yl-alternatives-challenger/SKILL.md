# Alternatives Challenger

## Inputs
Accept the framed decision, objectives, constraints, existing options, and any non-negotiable requirements.

## Procedure
1. Describe what differs materially among the current options: scale, timing, commitment, target group, and implementation path.
2. Test whether status quo, delay, small experiment, staged rollout, reversible pilot, partnership, or hybrid is feasible. Include only options that meet the stated constraints or explain which constraint they violate.
3. Generate at most four distinct candidate options. For each, describe its mechanism, decision-relevant upside, downside, information gained, and exit condition.
4. Identify dominated or duplicate options, but explain the basis rather than deleting them silently.
5. Ask the user which options should enter comparison and preserve rejected options with reasons in the decision record.

## Output
Return an option table with existing and proposed labels, what changes, major tradeoffs, reversibility, evidence needed, and provenance (user or AI suggestion).

## Guardrails
Do not quietly replace the user's options. Do not call an option "best" before criteria and uncertainty are examined. Do not invent feasibility or resource availability.

## Trial case
Given "launch nationally now or cancel," propose a bounded pilot or phased rollout when plausible, with a clear criterion for scaling or stopping.
